# Business hypothesis

Customer: freelancers and agencies responsible for 5–50 n8n workflows. They pay to reduce incident investigation effort, explain failures to clients, and avoid harmful duplicate work when retrying.

Current alternatives include n8n execution logs, generic observability, error alerts and manually consulting a general-purpose assistant. FlowMedic combines the error with stored evidence, durable investigation, human approval and a conservative retry policy in one workflow.

Proposed price: $29/month for a solo workspace, with a possible agency tier after interviews. This is a hypothesis, not launched billing or demonstrated willingness to pay. Self-hosted MIT source and local open-weight inference offer control over infrastructure and data handling; running and maintaining a model has real hardware costs.

Interview questions: How many workflows fail each week? Which failures take longest to investigate? What was your worst duplicate side effect? What information must accompany a diagnosis to trust it? Would local inference matter? How much would dependable incident triage be worth? Who approves recovery for client workflows?

Differentiation to validate: n8n-specific evidence, explanatory diagnosis, explicit uncertainty, durable approval and safe replay controls. Do not claim measured time savings, paying customers or production reliability without evidence.
